# Preliminary benchmark validation

Combined results from the two bodies of validation evidence, scored under the
current claim vocabulary. Generated by `analyse-validation.R` and
`make-figures.R` in this directory.

## The two bodies are not pooled

| | Body A | Body B |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Pérez-Losada course problem sets and quizzes | 10 published papers |
| Unit | 106 numeric checks | 57 extracted claims |
| Reference | the course's stated answer, known correct | none; the paper's assertion is what is tested |
| Question | does the implementation compute the same number? | does the published claim survive reanalysis? |
| Name | software verification | reproducibility assessment |

These measure different things. A single combined "validation accuracy" would
average a quantity with a known right answer against one without, so no such
figure is reported.

## Headline results

`tables/T1-headline.csv`. Intervals are Wilson score intervals, which behave
correctly at the boundary where the Wald interval collapses to zero width.

### Body A: course material

**90 of 98 scored checks agree (91.8%, 95% CI 84.7 to 95.8).**

The denominator excludes 8 of the 106 rows, each named rather than absorbed: 5
carry no expected value to agree with, 1 was refused by a guard, and 2 had no
input data on disk.

The eight disagreements are **not eight independent events**. They trace to three
documented causes, six of them to the same two decisions inside one problem set:

| Cause | Checks | Set |
|---|---|---|
| DESeq2 size factors: only 1 of 51 taxa is present in every sample | 3 | ps10 |
| ps10 Q2 passes an object that Q1 had overwritten with normalized counts | 3 | ps10 |
| Assumption check directs non-parametric; the question instructed ANOVA | 2 | ps7 |

Six of the eight sit in ps10 and two in ps7. Every other problem set agrees
completely. The interval above treats the 106 checks as independent, which they
are not, so it is narrower than the evidence supports. Treat it as descriptive.

### Body B: ten published papers

| Quantity | Result | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| Primary claim reproduced | 10/10 (100%) | 72.2 to 100 |
| Scorable claims reproduced | 30/40 (75.0%) | 59.8 to 85.8 |
| Claims adjudicable | 40/57 (70.2%) | 57.3 to 80.5 |

The 17 unscorable claims are reported by reason and never pooled: 7
`schema_gap`, 3 `not_attempted`, 3 `out_of_scope`, 2 `data_absent`, 2
`contrast_mismatch`.

**Adjudicability varies far more across studies than anything else measured**,
from 25% (Kostic) to 100% (Vogtmann, Feng, Pérez-Losada). A permutation test of
between-study homogeneity gives p = 0.06 for adjudicability and p = 0.49 for
reproduction among adjudicable claims. At ten studies this is suggestive and no
more, and the two standard deviations are similar (0.27 and 0.25).

## Robustness of the scoring rule

`tables/T3-threshold-sensitivity.csv`.

**No claim has a reanalysis p-value between 0.017 and 0.059.** The largest among
claims scored as reproduced is 0.017; the smallest among those scored as not
reproduced is 0.059. The 0.05 threshold is therefore not adjudicating a close
call in this set.

| alpha | reproduced | changed vs 0.05 |
|---|---|---|
| 0.001 | 19/35 | 9 |
| 0.01 | 27/35 | 1 |
| **0.05** | **28/35** | reference |
| 0.10 | 27/35 | 3 |
| 0.20 | 27/35 | 5 |

The result is stable between 0.01 and 0.05. Outside that range it moves, so the
threshold is a stated analytical choice rather than a neutral convention.

## What the rescore changed

Four claims moved from held to `not_reproduced` under the rule that a
directional claim requires significance and not only the right sign:

| Study | Claim | p |
|---|---|---|
| S2 Feng | *Bilophila wadsworthia* higher in carcinoma | 0.153 |
| S4 Kim | *Bacteroides* higher in reference | 0.059 |
| S4 Kim | Lachnospiraceae higher in reference | 0.518 |
| S5 Ramos | *Lactobacillus* higher under BPS | 0.955 |

Three claims moved from unadjudicated to `not_licensed`: the dispersion
downgrades in A0, A2 and A3. Those are findings about the papers and they now
carry their own status instead of sitting with missing data.

One item was **removed from the claim set entirely**: A0's "only 30% of published
taxa recovered". That is a concordance measure against a hard-coded 0.7
threshold, not a claim the paper made, and under the current scheme concordance
measures cannot decide an outcome.

`tables/T6-vocabulary-crosswalk.csv` gives the full old-to-new mapping.

F3 is faceted because the shaded region means opposite things for the two kinds
of claim. For a claim of difference, a non-significant reanalysis fails to
support it. For a claim of no difference, a non-significant reanalysis supports
it. Plotting both against one shaded region would misread half the data.

## What these numbers do not establish

1. **Not a failure rate for the literature.** The ten studies are a purposive
   coverage matrix chosen to exercise every input path, not a sample. No rate
   here describes microbiome papers in general.
2. **Not a contradiction of any paper.** Every claim scored `not_reproduced` is
   a failure to detect the asserted effect, not a significant effect in the
   opposite direction. Two of them (Kim's *Coprococcus* at p = 0.465 and
   *Eubacterium* at p = 0.662) point the other way but are nowhere near
   significance.
3. **Not a comparison of 16S against shotgun.** Fisher's exact test on scorable
   claims gives p = 0.72. With five studies per arm and claims clustered inside
   studies, the test has almost no power and is reported only to show that
   nothing in this set suggests a difference.
4. **Not traceable to run artifacts.** No `run_log.csv` or `tables/*.csv`
   survives from the ten studies, so the values in `claims-rescored.csv` were
   read from the written reports rather than from the runs. This violates the
   project's first standing rule and must be fixed before the main study.
