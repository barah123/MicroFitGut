# Preliminary benchmark validation

MicroFitGut is validated against two bodies of evidence. This directory holds the
combined results, the analysis that produced them, and the figures.

Generated by `analyse-validation.R` and `make-figures.R`, both seeded at 42.

## The two bodies are not pooled

| | Body A | Body B |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Pérez-Losada course problem sets and quizzes | 10 published papers |
| Unit | 106 numeric checks | 57 extracted claims |
| Reference | the course's stated answer, known correct | none; the paper's assertion is what is tested |
| Question | does the implementation compute the same number? | does the published claim survive reanalysis? |
| Name | software verification | reproducibility assessment |

These measure different things. A single combined validation accuracy would
average a quantity that has a known right answer against one that does not, so no
such figure is reported.

Headline values are in `tables/T1-headline.csv`. Intervals throughout are
Clopper-Pearson, which is conservative and behaves correctly at the boundary
where a Wald interval collapses to zero width.

## Body A: course material

| Level | Result | 95% Clopper-Pearson |
|---|---|---|
| **Per exercise question (headline)** | **28/34 = 82.4%** | 65.5 to 93.2 |
| Per row | 84/92 = 91.3% | 83.6 to 96.2 |

The exercise question is the analysis unit, because the 106 rows are not 106
independent checks. Several rows can derive from one fitted model, and two
structures in the file make the row-level count misleading:

1. **Seven `ps10 repro` rows are attribution, not independent successes.** They
   recompute the same six quantities under the course's own two choices, in order
   to demonstrate that those choices explain the discrepancy. Counting a
   discrepancy and its own explanation as separate data points inflates the
   denominator symmetrically, so the ratio holds steady while the sample size
   becomes fiction.
2. **One check appears twice.** `ps12 Q5` and `ps13 Q5` both report an AIC of
   117.9 against 117.913: the same fitted model, counted in two problem sets.

The denominator also excludes rows that are not checks at all, each named rather
than absorbed: 5 carry no expected value, 1 was refused by a guard, 2 had no input
data on disk. Per-set counts are in `tables/T4-coursework-by-set.csv`.

### The six disagreeing questions trace to three causes

| Cause | Questions | Set |
|---|---|---|
| DESeq2 size factors: only 1 of 51 taxa is present in every sample | Q2, Q3a, Q3b, Q3c | ps10 |
| `ps10 Q2` passes an object that Q1 had overwritten with normalized counts | same four | ps10 |
| Assumption check directs a non-parametric test; the question instructed ANOVA | Q6, Q8+ | ps7 |

Replicating the course's own choices reproduces its numbers exactly, which is what
attributes each divergence rather than merely noting it. Every problem set other
than ps10 and ps7 agrees completely. Full list in
`tables/T5-coursework-causes.csv`.

### What agreement means here

Most rows agree to the precision the answer key printed. Two are scored on the
conclusion rather than the value, which is the correct treatment for Monte Carlo
quantities and is a different criterion: `quiz13 Q1` compares intervals whose
lower bounds differ by 8.7%, and `quiz13 Q4` compares 0.74 against 0.81. Both
concern quantities that vary between runs of the same correct code.

## Body B: ten published papers

Fifty-seven claims were extracted from ten studies. Every claim carries exactly
one status, and only the four scorable statuses enter a denominator.

| Quantity | Result | 95% Clopper-Pearson |
|---|---|---|
| Scorable claims reproduced | 30/40 (75.0%) | 58.8 to 87.3 |
| Claims adjudicable | 40/57 (70.2%) | 56.6 to 81.6 |
| Designated headline claim reproduced | 10/10 (100%) | 69.2 to 100 |

Headline claims were designated during this analysis rather than in advance, so
the third row is descriptive of the designated set.

Of the 57 claims, **53 were scored by `score_claims()`** and 4 were adjudicated in
the report text. The `scored_by_function` column of `claims-scored.csv` marks
which is which, and the per-study counts reconcile with the run logs.

### The 17 claims outside the denominator

Reported by reason and never pooled, because they mean different things about
different objects: 7 `schema_gap`, 3 `not_attempted`, 3 `out_of_scope`, 2
`data_absent`, 2 `contrast_mismatch`. Counts in
`tables/T6-outside-the-denominator.csv`.

Only `not_attempted` is under the analyst's control. The rest describe what the
claim schema can express, what the paper measured, and what its deposit contains.

### Adjudicability varies more than anything else measured

From 25% (Kostic) to 100% (Vogtmann, Feng, Pérez-Losada). A permutation test of
between-study homogeneity gives p = 0.06 for adjudicability and p = 0.49 for
reproduction among adjudicable claims. At ten studies this is suggestive and no
more, and the two standard deviations are similar, 0.27 and 0.25. Per-study
values in `tables/T2-adjudicability-by-study.csv`.

## Robustness of the scoring rule

**No claim has a reanalysis p-value between 0.017 and 0.059.** The largest among
claims scored as reproduced is 0.017; the smallest among those scored as not
reproduced is 0.059. The 0.05 threshold therefore adjudicates no close call in
this set.

| alpha | reproduced | changed against 0.05 |
|---|---|---|
| 0.001 | 19/35 | 9 |
| 0.01 | 27/35 | 1 |
| **0.05** | **28/35** | reference |
| 0.10 | 27/35 | 3 |
| 0.20 | 27/35 | 5 |

The scored outcome is stable between 0.01 and 0.05, where one claim moves.
Outside that range it does move, so the threshold is a stated analytical choice
rather than a neutral convention. `tables/T3-threshold-sensitivity.csv`.

## What these numbers do not establish

1. **Not a failure rate for the literature.** The ten studies are a purposive
   coverage matrix chosen to exercise every input path the tool supports, not a
   sample. No rate here describes microbiome papers in general.
2. **Not a contradiction of any paper.** Every claim scored `not_reproduced` is a
   failure to detect the asserted effect, not a significant effect in the
   opposite direction. The two that point the other way, Kim's *Coprococcus* at
   p = 0.465 and *Eubacterium* at p = 0.662, are nowhere near significance.
3. **Not a comparison of 16S against shotgun.** Fisher's exact test on scorable
   claims gives p = 0.72, and the deeper problem is confounding rather than
   power. All five shotgun studies came from curated or pre-processed deposits,
   four through curatedMetagenomicData. The 16S arm is raw repository deposits,
   three of which were missing metadata, taxonomy, or a needed covariate. Three
   of the five 16S studies are also non-human. The 5 and 5 split is coverage,
   never a contrast.
4. **Not a test of guard specificity.** The set contains no study on which
   nothing fires, so it cannot separate guards that catch real problems from
   guards that fire on everything.
5. **Not an estimate for human-associated recent work.** Two of the ten are
   human-associated and published 2020 to 2025, and one of those is the tool's
   own development dataset. This does not weaken the coverage finding, which is
   what the matrix was built for, but it rules the set out as a basis for
   estimating anything about that population.
6. **Traceability is partial.** 39 run directories carry a `run_log.csv`, 293
   logged rows in total, so a logged value traces to its run. Result tables and
   the encoded claim objects were not retained. The values in `claims-scored.csv`
   were read from the study reports, and all 37 p-values were verified to appear
   verbatim in their source report, which confirms the transcription rather than
   the computation.

F3 is faceted because the shaded region means opposite things for the two kinds
of claim. For a claim of difference, a non-significant reanalysis fails to support
it. For a claim of no difference, a non-significant reanalysis supports it.
Plotting both against one shaded region would misread half the data.

### Colour

Every colour is a slot from a colourblind-validated categorical theme, and every
set was checked with a validator rather than chosen by eye. Full output in
`figures/PALETTE-VALIDATION.txt`.

| Role | Hex | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| reproduced, MATCH | `#2a78d6` | all-pairs CVD ΔE 13.0 (target ≥ 8), normal-vision 16.3 (floor 15), contrast ≥ 3:1 |
| not_reproduced, DIFFER | `#eb6834` | |
| not_licensed, BLOCKED | `#4a3aa7` | |
| not_tested | `#1baf7a` | four-set all-pairs CVD ΔE 9.2 |
| outside the denominator | `#aeaca4` | ordinal ramp, light end 2.21:1 |
| 16S / Shotgun | `#008300` / `#e87ba4` | CVD ΔE 17.6 |

Three choices are recorded because each was a trade rather than a default:

1. **Green and red are not used for reproduced against not reproduced.** Every
   ordering of that pairing measures CVD ΔE 7.2, inside the band that is legal
   only with additional relief; blue and orange reach 13.0. Polarity is carried
   by legend order and labels, so identity never rests on colour alone.
2. **The five unscorable reasons fold to one grey in F1.** Nine colour classes in
   a stacked bar exceeds what any palette separates. They have their own figure,
   F6, where they can be read.
3. **Technology is green and magenta, not blue and orange.** Blue already means
   reproduced in three panels of the composite, and a colour keeps one meaning
   across a figure set.

The figures target a light surface (`#fcfcfb`). A journal prints on white and the
README embeds the same raster, so no dark variant is claimed.

**Table view.** `claims-scored.csv` and `validation-results.xlsx` carry every
plotted value without colour.
